Key takeaways:

1/ Financial innovation is a double-edged sword. The same features that make payments faster, cheaper, and more accessible also create new channels for criminals and terrorist financiers.

2/ Crypto-assets are now at the center of this new risk landscape. Transactions are executed in seconds, cross borders without friction, and provide access to global liquidity with a single click. Many of these tools let users hide who they are.

3/ According to the FATF, stablecoins are increasingly becoming the preferred tool of terrorist financiers. They preserve value, move instantly across borders, and are easy to access, which is exactly why they are being exploited.

4/ Uneven implementation is, in effect, no implementation at all. FATF standards must be applied consistently across jurisdictions. Otherwise, criminals will always find the weakest link.

5/ Türkiye acted early and decisively on crypto-related AML/CFT risks. In 2021, we brought VASPs under AML/CFT obligations, implemented the travel rule, and introduced licensing and compliance requirements. We also banned crypto ATMs outright, as the risks were simply too high.

6/ Criminal networks move funds in seconds. International cooperation must move at the same speed. When a suspicious wallet is identified, information must travel in hours, not weeks.

Financial innovation will continue to evolve, and so will the risks. Strong international cooperation remains essential.
